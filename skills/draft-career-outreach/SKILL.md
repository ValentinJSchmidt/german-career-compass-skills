# Draft Career Outreach

Write the thing that gets a conversation started. Which form that takes depends on the
option's `visibility` marker — and for a lot of work, **the right answer is a phone call,
not a letter**.

## Prerequisites

Requires the person's profile, the chosen option with its `visibility` and contact path, and
whatever evidence about the employer was verified during discovery.

## Pick the form first

| Situation | What to write |
|---|---|
| `posted_online` — a real advertisement | An application answering that advertisement specifically |
| `employer_page_only` — verified employer, no posting | A short Initiativbewerbung, plus a call script for following it up |
| `offline_likely` — this work is rarely advertised | **A phone script first.** A written message second, and only if the person prefers it |
| Track (a) — staying with the current employer | A script for the development conversation, and a short written follow-up |

Do not default to a formal cover letter. For a twelve-person Handwerk firm, a polished
three-paragraph Anschreiben is the wrong instrument: the owner wants to know who is calling,
what they can do, and when they can start. For a large employer with a portal, the formal
route is the only one that works. **Match the instrument to the employer.**

## Workflow

1. Read `references/outreach-rubric.md`.
2. Confirm what was actually verified about this employer. Do not reference anything that
   was not opened during discovery.
3. Draft the form chosen above, keeping it short — see the length rules in the rubric.
4. Lead with what the person can **do**, with evidence, not with what they want.
5. Handle the awkward parts plainly: a gap, a change of direction, a missing certificate.
   One honest sentence beats an elaborate explanation, and beats hiding it.
6. Offer one shorter variant.
7. For phone scripts, include what to say if the answer is "we're not hiring" — that is the
   most common answer and where most people hang up too early.

## Output

Always include, in this order:

1. **An AI-generated notice**, above the draft: say plainly that this is AI-generated, that
   it should not be sent as-is, that the person should rewrite at least the opening and
   closing in their own words, cut anything they could not defend in an interview, and check
   every factual claim about themselves and the employer.
2. **The draft** — the message, or the call script with the opening line, the two or three
   things to say, and the closing question.
3. **Why this is specific** — one sentence.
4. **What to check before sending or calling** — name, spelling, whether the posting is still
   live, best time to call.
5. **What happens next** — what a normal reply or non-reply looks like here, and when to
   follow up.

## Rules

- Never overstate skills, availability, qualifications, or prior relationships.
- Never invent an opening, a contact name, a shared connection, or a reason for applying.
- Never claim a certificate the person does not have. If the ad asks for one they lack, say
  so in one line and lead with the evidence that compensates.
- Never write a message that commits the person to hours, shifts, or a start date they told
  you they cannot do.
- Keep the AI-generated notice, always — including when the person asks only for the text,
  or for the short version. The notice is for the person, not for the employer: do not put a
  disclosure of AI authorship into the message itself unless they ask for one.
- Do not send anything, submit any form, or contact anyone. Drafting only.
- Mirror the person's language. If they will send it in German, write it in German, at a
  register they would actually use — a letter that does not sound like them fails at the
  interview.
