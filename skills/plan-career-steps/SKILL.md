# Plan Career Steps

Turn one chosen option into something a person can start on Monday. The plan must survive
contact with their actual finances, hours, and obligations — otherwise it is a wish.

## Prerequisites

Requires the six-dimension profile and one chosen option from `find-career-options`,
including its `visibility` marker and contact path.

The **income floor** and **survivable months of reduced income** are hard inputs. If they
are not in the profile, ask before planning.

Requires live web/search access to verify training routes, durations, costs, and current
programme names.

## Workflow

1. Restate the goal in one sentence and the deadline pressure behind it.
2. Read `references/german-training-routes.md` and pick the **shortest honest route**, not
   the most complete qualification. Several routes usually reach the same job with very
   different demands on time and money.
3. Build the **income bridge before the steps** — see the section below. A plan that does not
   say how the rent gets paid is not a plan.
4. Sequence the steps. Each step gets: what to do, who to contact, roughly how long, what it
   costs, and what has to be true before the next step starts.
5. Front-load the steps that are free, reversible, and fast — a chamber consultation, a
   phone call, a trial day, an evening course. Put the irreversible commitments after the
   cheap checks that could invalidate them.
6. Read `references/funding-routes.md` and name the funding routes worth **checking**. Never
   state that someone qualifies for anything.
7. For options marked `offline_likely`, give a concrete offline action: who to call, what to
   say, which chamber or Innung holds the member list, whether a walk-in is normal in this
   trade.
8. Verify durations, costs, and programme names live. Do not carry them from memory.
9. Name what could derail this and what to check first.

## The income bridge

**Every plan names how the person lives through it.** No plan may propose leaving paid work
before the bridge exists.

Work through, in this order:

1. **Stay employed if at all possible.** Evening or part-time training, internal
   progression, or Weiterbildung alongside the job costs the least and risks the least.
2. **Modular routes.** A Teilqualifikation makes someone employable after each module rather
   than only at the end. For a short runway this is usually the correct answer.
3. **Part-time work during training**, where the training schedule allows it.
4. **Existing entitlements** — only ever as "check whether this applies to you", with the
   office that decides it named.
5. **Household sources** — savings, partner income, reduced costs — only if the person
   raised them.

Then do the arithmetic out loud against their stated floor: how many months at what income,
and whether that fits inside what they said is survivable. **If it does not fit, say so and
change the plan** — a shorter route, a part-time variant, a staged version — rather than
presenting a plan that quietly requires more runway than they have.

**Never advise resigning to become eligible for anything.** Voluntary resignation can affect
benefit entitlement in Germany, and the rules are not something to reason about from memory.
Tell the person to speak to the Agentur für Arbeit **before** giving notice, and say why.

## Output

- **The goal** — one sentence, and the deadline behind it
- **The route** — which of the training routes, and why this one rather than the longer one
- **The income bridge** — how the money works, month by month, against their floor
- **The steps** — numbered, sequenced, each with contact, duration, cost, and precondition
- **This week** — the two or three things to do in the next seven days, including the phone
  calls
- **Funding to check** — programme names, who decides, what to bring, framed as questions to
  ask and never as entitlements
- **What could derail this** — the two or three realistic risks, and the cheap check for each
- **What I could not verify** — stated plainly

## Rules

- Never state that someone is eligible for a benefit, a funding programme, or a place. Name
  the office that decides and what to ask. Eligibility depends on individual circumstances
  and current rules, and getting it wrong costs the person real money.
- Never invent costs, durations, training places, or programme names. Verify live, or mark
  as unverified.
- Never advise quitting before the bridge exists, and never advise quitting to unlock a
  benefit.
- No legal, tax, medical, or financial determinations. Point to the body that gives them.
- Respect the constraints as filters: a plan that requires evening classes when the person
  has a fixed care window at 17:00 is not a plan.
- Prefer the shortest honest route. Do not default to the full qualification because it is
  more complete.
- Keep it small enough to start. Three real steps beat a twelve-step programme nobody begins.
