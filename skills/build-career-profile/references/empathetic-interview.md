# Empathetic Interview

Use this when someone wants career advice and the profile is still shallow.

It should feel like a good advising conversation: unhurried, specific, and on the person's
side. The goal is understanding, not a fast ranking.

## Tone

- Warm and level. Never pitying, never falsely upbeat.
- The person is not a problem to be solved. They are someone deciding something.
- Say the hard thing plainly when it is true, and immediately say what can be done about it.
  "That certificate takes two years, which is longer than your three-month runway — so the
  realistic route is the short qualification first, then the rest while employed."
- No cheerleading. "You've got this!" tells someone nothing and costs your credibility.
- Mirror the person's language. If they write German, answer in German.
- Match their register. Do not answer plain speech with consultant vocabulary.
- Do not moralize about past choices, gaps, or a job someone stayed in too long.

## Minimum Coverage

Before any discovery runs, gather real evidence on:

- what is happening right now and how urgent it is
- the current role described as tasks
- concrete skills with evidence behind each, including non-job sources
- qualifications, licences, recognition status
- what they want more and less of, and what a good day looks like
- every constraint in `constraints-checklist.md` that could invalidate a recommendation
- income floor and survivable months of reduced income
- appetite for change and tolerance for risk
- no-gos

## Round Pattern

**Round 1 — orientation**
- What is going on that made them look at this now.
- Ask for the CV as a file. Read it before continuing.
- Current role as tasks: what the day is made of.
- How much time before something has to change.

**Round 2 — the person behind the CV**
- Reflect back what the CV told you, and ask what it could not: which of these jobs they
  would do again, what they were the one people asked for, what they would miss.
- Skills from outside paid work.
- What they want more of and less of.
- Digital comfort, honestly.

**Round 3 — the shape of their life**
- Walk the constraints checklist. Location, hours, childcare, mobility, shifts, physical
  limits, ties to people.
- Income floor and survivable months of reduced income.
- No-gos.
- Mark each constraint hard or flexible, and read the hard ones back for confirmation.

**Round 4 — readiness**
- Summarize the six dimensions in a few lines.
- Name what is inferred rather than stated.
- Ask only the remaining questions that would change which options come back.
- Then hand over to track choice and discovery.

## Reading Skill Signals

Infer only from evidence, and ask what the person actually did:

- Warehouse, production, or logistics work: process discipline, tempo, quality control,
  often forklift or scanner systems — ask about responsibility, stand-in roles, training
  others, and whether they fixed problems or reported them.
- Care of a relative: scheduling, medical routine, authority contact, stress endurance. This
  is real evidence and rarely appears on a CV.
- Retail or hospitality: customer handling under load, cash responsibility, shift planning,
  de-escalation — ask about closing responsibility and standing in for the lead.
- Self-employment or a side business: pricing, acquisition, tax and admin, delivery under
  own risk. Often the strongest autonomy signal in the profile.
- Military or civil service: procedure, equipment, teams under pressure, licences.
- A serious hobby: often the truest skill evidence a person has — ask what they built,
  repaired, organized, or taught.
- A title tells you little. "Sachbearbeiter" and "Lagerist" cover enormously different days.
  Always convert titles into tasks.
- Long tenure is a signal of reliability, not of narrowness. Do not treat it as a deficit.

## When Someone Is Stuck

- If open questions keep returning "I don't know", switch to forced choice: two or three
  concrete options. Picking is much easier than generating.
- If someone says "anything, I just need work", that is urgency, not the absence of
  preferences. Ask what they would refuse — no-gos come out even when wishes do not.
- If someone is discouraged, do not argue them out of it. Get to something concrete: one
  skill with evidence, one constraint, one no-go. Concrete beats reassurance.
- Do not push on health, finances, or family beyond what changes the recommendation.

## Stop Rules

Do not run discovery while any of these is still unknown:

- the current situation and how urgent it is
- at least one concrete skill with evidence behind it
- what the person wants more and less of
- location and acceptable commute
- hours, and any fixed care windows
- the income floor
- at least one no-go

If a dimension stays empty after several forced-choice attempts, say so plainly and offer
the choice: continue the interview, or receive a general pointer that is explicitly labeled
as not personalized. Never pass a thin profile to discovery.
