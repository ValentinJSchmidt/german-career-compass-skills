# Constraints Checklist

These are the facts that decide whether a recommendation is real or useless. A CV contains
none of them. Walk this list before discovery; do not guess an answer.

Mark every item **hard** or **flexible**. Discovery filters on hard constraints and may only
relax them out loud, with consent, via
`find-career-options/references/self-correction-ladder.md`.

## Place

- Where do you live (town or postcode)?
- How far are you willing to travel each day — in km, in minutes, or "as far as the bus goes"?
- By what transport: own car, licence but no car, public transport, bike, on foot?
- If by rail: do you have a Deutschland-Ticket, and is long-distance rail (ICE/IC) something
  you could actually pay for daily? Usually not — in that case the regional connection is the
  real commute time.
- Would you move? If not, is that about family, friends, a flat, a partner's job, or care duties?
- Remote, hybrid, or onsite — and is remote actually possible at home (space, connection, quiet)?

Ask the "would you move" question **without pressure**. Staying near friends and family is a
legitimate, common, and often correct choice. Never treat it as a limitation to be overcome.

## Time

- Hours per week: full-time, part-time, and how many hours exactly?
- Fixed windows that cannot move — daycare, school, pickup times, care for a relative,
  therapy, custody arrangements?
- Shifts: early, late, night, weekend, on-call, rotating — which are acceptable?
- Notice period in the current job?
- How much time before something has to change?

## Money

- **Income floor** — the rough monthly net the household needs. A rough number is enough.
- How many months of reduced or lost income are survivable?
- Money available for training or certificates, if any?
- Existing obligations that make a gap impossible (rent, credit, maintenance payments)?

Ask plainly and without apology. Do not push for exact finances, and do not comment on them.

## Body and Health

Only as far as it affects what work is realistic:

- Lifting and carrying limits?
- Standing, sitting, or kneeling for long periods?
- Noise, dust, cold, heat, screens?
- Shift work and sleep?
- Stress load and customer contact?

Do not ask for a diagnosis, do not interpret one, and do not give medical advice. Record only
the working limit: "no lifting over 10 kg", "no night shifts".

## Paper

- Work permit or residence status, where relevant?
- Language level, honestly — spoken and written, German and others?
- Licences: driving classes, forklift, Sachkundenachweis, care, food hygiene, safety passes?
- Formal qualifications, and for foreign ones, the recognition status
  (anerkannt, teilweise anerkannt, nicht beantragt)?
- Anything that legally blocks a field (register entries, health certificates for food or
  care work)?

## People

- Who depends on you, and when?
- Is there someone whose schedule yours must fit around?
- What would the people around you have to absorb if this changed?

## Using the answers

- Hard constraints are **filters**, applied before ranking — not tie-breakers afterwards.
- Never put personal or sensitive constraint wording into a web search query. Convert it:
  "no night shifts because of the kids" becomes a shift-pattern filter, not a query.
- If a constraint makes the field thin, name the constraint and the size of the gap, and let
  the person choose what to do. Never quietly drop one.
